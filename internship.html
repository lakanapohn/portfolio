My individual contribution

Building a repeatable
soil-moisture workflow.

During my internship at PeaceEye FlexCo, I supported Earth Observation and geospatial projects for environmental applications. My main responsibility was the soil-moisture component of a wider soil-suitability project for SOS Children’s Villages Somalia.

Using Bonkay Main Farm near Baidoa as a pilot, I developed a reproducible workflow combining Sentinel-1 radar with Sentinel-2 optical indicators, rainfall, and terrain information. The work moved from literature review and method testing to monthly maps, sensitivity analysis, and technical handover.

The project context

Monitoring a farm
with Earth Observation.

The wider project aims to combine satellite observations, field measurements, laboratory analysis, and IoT sensors to support soil and agricultural decision-making. Soil moisture, organic carbon, and salinity are intended components of an integrated soil-suitability assessment.

My work focused on preliminary spatial and temporal moisture conditions. Without field measurements for calibration, I used a relative approach that compares each pixel with its own historical radar behaviour.

The maps show relative moisture conditions. They do not measure absolute volumetric soil moisture.

Literature review

I reviewed EO-based soil-moisture methods, including Sentinel-1 approaches and combinations with optical and environmental information. The review informed the choice of radar as the main signal and highlighted the need for field calibration before estimating absolute moisture.

Vegetation, surface roughness, and acquisition geometry can also affect backscatter. These factors shaped the data selection, consistent orbit choice, and interpretation of the outputs. The scientific references are listed at the end of this page.

Data selection

Each dataset has a different role and scale. I selected radar for relative surface-moisture monitoring, optical data for vegetation and surface context, rainfall for temporal interpretation, and terrain for supporting spatial information.

Sentinel-1 · Processing & RSM equation

Sentinel-1 SAR GRD is the main moisture dataset. The workflow uses IW-mode VV/VH imagery, a consistent descending pass, and relative orbit 108. VV is used for RSM; VH provides supporting information.

  1. Check acquisition availability and use the 2021–2025 observations as the historical reference.
  2. Use the preprocessed Earth Engine GRD product, with calibration and terrain correction already applied.
  3. Convert VV from decibels to linear backscatter and apply a 3 × 3 median filter.
  4. Exclude mapped buildings and derive the 10th and 90th percentiles for each pixel’s reference series.
  5. Scale each 2026 observation against those dry and wet references, constrain RSM to 0–1, and produce monthly pixel-wise median maps.
VVlinear=10VVdB10
RSM=VVcurrent−VVdryVVwet−VVdry

VVdry = P10 and VVwet = P90 of the historical linear backscatter series. A value near 0 is relatively dry and a value near 1 relatively wet compared with the same pixel’s past behaviour. This is not volumetric soil moisture in m³/m³.

Sentinel-2 · Screening, indices & monthly maps

Harmonized Sentinel-2 Level-2A surface reflectance provides vegetation and surface context. B2, B3, B4, and B8 are 10 m bands; B11 is a 20 m band aligned for the analysis.

  1. Apply scene-classification screening, retaining vegetation, bare soil, and water. Keep observations with at least 20% clear coverage over the farm.
  2. Scale stored reflectance values by 0.0001.
  3. Calculate NDVI, NDMI, BSI, and MNDWI using the equations below.
  4. Create monthly mean and median composites, align them to the Sentinel-1 grid, clip the farm, and exclude buildings.
  5. Keep missing observations as NoData. June 2026 did not supply a suitable input for the final MCA.
NDVI=B8−B4B8+B4
NDMI=B8−B11B8+B11
BSI=(B11+B4)−(B8+B2)(B11+B4)+(B8+B2)
MNDWI=B3−B11B3+B11

B2 = blue, B3 = green, B4 = red, B8 = near infrared, and B11 = short-wave infrared. NDVI describes greenness, NDMI canopy moisture, BSI bare-soil conditions, and MNDWI wet-surface context. MNDWI is not a primary criterion in the MCA.

CHIRPS · Rainfall comparison with RSM

CHIRPS Daily v2 supplies precipitation in mm/day at 0.05° resolution, approximately 5.6 km near Bonkay. I extracted rainfall at the farm centroid, checked availability, calculated monthly totals, and matched recent rainfall with Sentinel-1 observation dates.

Antecedent totals cover the previous 1, 3, 7, and 10 days, excluding the satellite observation day. For a window of n days:

An(t)=∑k=1nR(t−k)

R is daily rainfall and t is the radar observation date. The ten-day comparison is shown below the Sentinel-2 maps. Rainfall is regional temporal context, not a detailed spatial criterion within the farm.

Terrain · Preparation & alignment

I prepared approximately 30 m elevation data in UTM zone 38N, derived elevation, slope, aspect, and hillshade, and assessed terrain variability. Elevation and slope were aligned to the Sentinel-1 10 m grid for integration.

The farm is relatively flat. Resampling supports alignment; it does not create finer terrain information from the original elevation data.

Method creation

I selected a relative change-detection approach because field calibration data were not available. The method scales current VV backscatter between pixel-specific P10 and P90 references rather than relying on noisy absolute minima and maxima.

I tested consistent radar geometry, historical reference periods, temporal aggregation, and supporting optical indices. Rainfall remained temporal context, while suitable spatial indicators were prepared for integration. Open Sentinel-1 in Data selection to see the RSM calculation.

GEE processing

  1. Filter Sentinel-1 to the selected mode, polarization, descending pass, and relative orbit. Prepare VV and calculate the historical references and RSM images.
  2. Screen Sentinel-2 quality, scale reflectance, and calculate the optical indices.
  3. Prepare daily CHIRPS records and rainfall summaries.
  4. Generate monthly products and export tables and GeoTIFFs for further processing.

Google Earth Engine handles data access and satellite processing. The exported results support local checks, figures, and later integration.

Local processing

I loaded exported products into Python and Google Colab, checked coordinate systems, alignment, missing values, and observation counts, and prepared monthly statistics, maps, and time-series figures.

Reusable CSV, GeoTIFF, PNG, and metadata outputs reduced repeated processing. The workflow documents dates, farm boundaries, masks, and parameters so another analyst can update the analysis.

Integrate / MCA preparation

  1. Use a common Sentinel-1 10 m grid and the same farm and building masks.
  2. Retain pixels valid in all required monthly criteria. Missing values stay NoData.
  3. Standardize RSM, NDMI, NDVI, BSI, and slope to 0–1. Reverse the BSI and slope criteria so higher standardized values represent more favourable moisture conditions.
  4. Apply preliminary weights and compare alternative scenarios, including a model without RSM.
Availability=∑i=15wici
Risk=1−Availability

c is a standardized criterion and w its weight. The RSM65 baseline uses 65% RSM, 15% NDMI, 10% NDVI, 5% reversed BSI, and 5% reversed slope. I also tested RSM50 balanced, RSM75 heavy, and equal weighting. These are assumptions for preliminary screening, not validated optimal weights.

Results

The workflow produces monthly maps, time series, relative moisture-availability and water-stress-risk indicators, and sensitivity comparisons. The maps distinguish relatively wetter and drier conditions while preserving observation gaps.

Interpretation & next steps

A foundation for
future field validation.

Field and IoT validation had not yet been completed. Vegetation and surface roughness can influence radar backscatter, while clouds create gaps in the optical observations. The small farm contains relatively few 10 m pixels, and the weights and class limits remain preliminary. Irrigation, drainage, and evaporation were not directly modelled.

The next steps are to match field measurements with satellite acquisitions, refine weights and thresholds, integrate soil organic carbon and salinity, and test the workflow on additional farms and seasons.
