My BIP experience

Learning to read
a changing coast.

I attended the CIVIS Blended Intensive Programme on climate change and coastal landscape evolution in the Mediterranean context. Learning about the geomorphology of Naxos helped me understand how coastal landscapes develop, and how strongly climate change can affect them.

What stayed with me was the connection between understanding a landscape and caring for its future—from nature-based solutions to the role geoinformatics can play in monitoring change. Experiencing the local culture was also an important part of the visit.

The course context

From online learning
to the island landscape.

The programme combines online preparation with field and laboratory activities in the Cyclades. Coastal landforms, erosion, sea-level indicators, lagoons, dunes, and human influence on the coast provide a setting for exploring climate change and landscape evolution.

The course connects these subjects with methods such as GIS mapping, aerial photogrammetry, and RTK-GNSS measurements, alongside discussion of coastal vulnerability and nature-based solutions.

Field notes · Geology & island history

Reading the landscape,
from ancient quarries to today’s coast.

Kouros of Melanes

Near the village of Melanes, the unfinished colossal Kouros remains in the ancient marble quarry where it was abandoned during the Archaic period. This encounter connects geology, landscape, and human activity: the geological setting of Naxos directly influenced ancient artistic production and quarrying practices.

Palatia & the Portara

On the small islet of Palatia, the Portara marks the unfinished Temple of Apollo. A sequence of deposits left by rivers and torrents provides evidence of wetter climatic conditions in the past—showing how geology can help us read environmental history alongside cultural landmarks.
