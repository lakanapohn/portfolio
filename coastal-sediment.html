Landscape Architecture · Bachelor’s Degree Project

Coastal Sediment Improvement
for Mahachai Mangrove

Design & improvement of Mahachai Mangrove

Working with tides,
sediment, and mangroves.

This landscape architecture proposal explores how sediment improvement and mangrove restoration can support a changing shoreline. The design brings coastal protection, ecological research, and public learning together at the Mahachai Gulf Coast Nature Study Center.

Location
Bang Ya Phraek, Samut Sakhon, Thailand
Project
Conceptual landscape design during my bachelor’s degree
Focus
Coastal erosion, mangrove ecosystems, and community learning

Site & coastal change

A vulnerable edge
between land and sea.

The project studies a coastline where mangroves, aquaculture, waterways, and settlement meet. Coastal erosion and damaged infrastructure form the starting point for a proposal that combines ecosystem restoration with places to study and understand the shoreline.

The regional map sets the site within the Bangkok Metropolitan Region and the Gulf of Thailand. Its sea-level scenarios are part of the original project analysis.

Design strategy

A coastline built in layers.

The proposal combines mangrove preservation, nursery preparation, and a series of bamboo structures along the intertidal edge. The sediment tools explore different arrangements for moderating wave action and encouraging sediment to accumulate, creating conditions for future habitat restoration.

01

Angled bamboo jetties

Bamboo structures arranged at a 60° angle form one of the proposed sediment improvement tools.

02

Strip-line bamboo fences

Pyramidal bamboo fences are paired with small-scale aquatic farming in the second configuration.

03

An integrated arrangement

Zigzagging bamboo jetties, pyramidal fences, and a netting trap bring the tools together.

Research & community

Restoration as a place to learn.

The Nature Study Center is conceived as a hub for studying coastal environmental diversity. Research spaces and boardwalks connect restoration work with public learning, helping visitors understand the value of mangroves and giving the community a place to engage with environmental conservation.
